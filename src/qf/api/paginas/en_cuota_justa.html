What fair odds are

The fair odds for an outcome are the inverse of its probability: if an outcome has a probability of 50%, its fair odds are 2.00; if it has 25%, they are 4.00. It is plain arithmetic: fair odds = 1 / probability, and the reverse: probability = 1 / odds. "Fair" simply means there is no margin added.

Why bookmaker odds do not add up to 100%

If you convert every price in a market using 1 / odds and add them up, the total is over 100%. That excess is the bookmaker's margin (also called the vig or overround): it is what the bookmaker charges for offering the market. The larger the sum, the wider the margin.

How the margin is removed

  • Proportional. Divides each implied probability by the total sum. It is the simplest and most common method, though it tends to overstate longshots.
  • Shin. Assumes part of the money wagered comes from better-informed bettors and estimates that share. It usually adjusts longshots better than proportional scaling. QuantFut uses Shin to compare its probabilities against closing market prices, as explained in our methodology.
  • Power. Raises each implied probability to a common exponent chosen so that the probabilities sum to 100%.

None of these methods reveals the "true" probability: they are practical ways to estimate the probability implied behind a price. Nor do they turn any price into a recommendation.
